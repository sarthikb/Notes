## 💥 The Problem with Push Model for High-Follower Users

Let’s say **User A** has **10 million followers**, and they post a new photo:

### 🔁 In Push (Fan-out) Model:

* The system **writes that post ID** to **10 million individual feeds**
* That means:

  * 10 million **write operations**
  * Potential load spikes on FeedService
  * High I/O cost, potential queuing delays
  * Storage bloat — even inactive users get updates they might never read

---

## 🧠 Why That’s a Problem

| Issue               | Result                           |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Write amplification | Slower write time, infra strain  |
| Wasted resources    | Updates pushed to inactive users |
| Difficult to scale  | Becomes bottleneck at high scale |

---

## ✅ So What Does Instagram Do?

They **detect high-follower users** and **avoid pushing their posts** into feeds.

Instead:

* Store their posts in the **main post database**
* When a follower opens the app, their **FeedService pulls** posts from:

  * Their own feed (for normal users' posts)
  * The post DB (for high-follower users like celebrities)
* This creates a **hybrid model**:

  * **Push** for regular users
  * **Pull** for viral / celebrity accounts

This balances:

* **Read efficiency** for most users
* **Write scalability** for high-follower users

---

## 🎯 Summary You Can Remember

> "Push when small, pull when big."

* Push → better latency, more infra cost
* Pull → cheaper to write, more load on read
* Hybrid → best of both
